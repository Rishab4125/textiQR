# TextiQR
## About
The textile and apparel industry faces significant challenges due to the prevalence of duplicate and counterfeit products. These issues compromise both the trustworthiness and authenticity of branded items. QR codes have emerged as a widely adopted solution for product authentication, offering enhanced transparency and detailed product information. However, in the textile sector, QR codes are typically printed on external tags that are attached to garments. These tags are easily replicable and replaceable, which undermines their effectiveness and raises concerns about their reliability as a means of authentication.

To address this, my research focuses on the direct printing or weaving of QR codes onto fabrics and garments themselves. This approach offers a more secure and tamper-proof method for embedding authentication markers, potentially enhancing consumer trust and creating a more integrated and seamless user experience. However, this method presents substantial technical challenges. The deformable and textured nature of fabrics introduces a range of scanning difficulties, including geometric and perspective distortions, thread-level interference, shadows, and various forms of visual noise. These factors often render traditional mobile and handheld QR scanners ineffective in real-world conditions.

TextiQR is a novel solution that tackles these limitations by incorporating advanced image preprocessing techniques, including filtering, thresholding, and Thin Plate Spline-based perspective correction. In addition, it leverages YOLO-based object segmentation, inspired by the ERIC CANAS model, to robustly detect and isolate QR codes in complex textile environments. Through this hybrid approach, TextiQR delivers a state-of-the-art scanning system designed specifically for QR codes embedded directly on textile surfaces.
